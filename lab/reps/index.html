The app

Counting reps, on the phone

An experiment, not a feature. It watches one measurement — where your hips are, how far apart your hands are — and counts the swings. Everything happens on this device: no video is uploaded, nothing is recorded, nothing is saved.

Press start. It will ask for the camera.

That line is the measurement it is counting. The two faint rails are the thresholds a rep has to cross. If the count is wrong, this shows why — a flat line means the movement is too small or you are out of frame, a jagged one means it is losing track of you.

What this is testing

Counting reps does not need good pose estimation — it needs one honest periodic signal. Depth can be noisy and a forearm can flip without changing where your hips are over time. That is why this is a day of work and judging your form is months.

Each exercise here is a recipe: which point to watch, how far it has to travel, and how long to wait before another rep can count. Adding a movement is about fifteen lines, not a new model. Four are here to prove that rather than assert it.

Known limits: it needs your whole body in frame in reasonable light, it cannot run while you are on a video call because the camera is busy, and it will not work for carries, planks, or anything that leaves the frame.
